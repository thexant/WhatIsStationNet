Welcome to StationNet

A StationNet station is a small, independent online place run by a person or community. Open it in your browser to leave a bulletin, talk with other members, share a file, tune the radio, or see what someone has built.

It brings back a good idea from early computing: the network can feel like a place with regulars, local character, and somebody at the controls.

> A familiar idea, on a newer screen

WHAT IT IS

Before social feeds filled the web, people called into bulletin board systems, or BBSs: computers that hosted messages, files, games, and conversations for their own communities. StationNet takes that sense of a shared local hangout and makes it available through a modern browser.

It also borrows from radio, where you tune to a frequency and meet whoever is there, and from projects like PirateBox and LibraryBox, which showed how a small local network could share useful things without depending on a big central service and constant connection to the world wide web. You don't need a radio license or old computer to enjoy the idea: visit an address, enter a station, and look around.

// IN PLAIN LANGUAGE //

A station is the software running on someone's computer, plus the people and material they choose to host there. Its operator is the Sysop—short for system operator.

[01] OWNED BY ITS HOST

When the internet was a place

Messages and shared material belong to a particular station, giving its community a sense of where it gathers.

[02] RUN BY PEOPLE

Local character

A Sysop chooses the name, rules, services, and atmosphere. A quiet reading room and a noisy game night can both be StationNet.

[03] REACHABLE BY BROWSER

Easy to enter

Visitors use a web browser. Hosts can start locally without a domain, cloud account, Docker, or external database.

STATION SERVICES

People & conversation

01 / 04

Read or post bulletins, talk in public chat, send private mail, browse member profiles and feeds, or check announcements and events. There is also a shared file archive and an optional imageboard.

Games & applications

02 / 04

Explore installable games and utilities, including programs made for StationNet. Stations can offer a MUD- a text-based multiplayer world where you travel, meet characters, and take on adventures.

Media & collections

03 / 04

A station may run an arcade for classic games, a video collection, an EPUB and PDF library, or a local wiki of offline reference material. The content comes from that station's operator and members.

Tools & creativity

04 / 04

Optional Office tools include documents, sheets, presentations, an agenda, and drawing. Members can also make music with a MIDI composer or write BASIC programs that can become StationNet games and apps.

GOOD TO KNOW: A Sysop can enable, disable, and configure services. You will not find every feature on every station.

> Tune in, then say hello

THE RADIO ROOM

StationNet's radio is a shared room with frequencies instead of channels in a feed. Depending on a station's settings and your permissions, you can listen, key out Morse code, send simple slow-scan pictures, or use push-to-talk voice. Authorized hosts can put together music and spoken programs for other members to hear.

It is intentionally local to a station. It is a way for people there to make a little noise together and communicate creatively. Live voice depends on a browser connection and suitable network setup.

> Many stations, many atmospheres

NO CENTRAL HUB REQUIRED

StationNet is software people host for themselves. Each station has its own accounts, content, settings, rules, and Sysop. One might be open to a neighborhood; another might be an invitation-only place for friends. The same software can feel very different from one address to the next.

There is no official master list of stations. You might learn about one from a friend, a local group, or a community that shares its address. Some stations are only reachable on the same local network; others may be made available more widely by their operators. If the station you want does not exist yet, you can run it.

// THE SYSOP'S DESK //

The Sysop decides who can join, which services appear, what members can share, and how the station looks. Colours, banners, icons, and station messages can all give it its own identity.
